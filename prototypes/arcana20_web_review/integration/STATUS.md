## Quality and measurement

An internal session-only cadence selector offers High (60 Hz source ticks), Balance (up to 30 Hz) and Performance (up to 20 Hz) outside protected event windows. This is **not an approved or shippable set of quality tiers**. Reduced cadence changes visual motion, and switching/overlapping casts and hardware profiling still require verification. In limited fixed-step samples, the High tick input matched raw-seconds output for B, C and D source players. The source-only #20 synthetic CPU benchmark rendered 60/30/20 samples with medians 228.7/115.3/78.4 ms and identical final fields; it does not measure game FPS or visual equivalence.

D19 High remains unbatched by default; experimental Balance and Performance use sprite-only batching, with `arcanaDBatch=none` restoring the unbatched path and `arcanaDBatch=sprites` opting High in. The candidate produced byte-identical outputs in ten paired SwiftShader samples and reduced one sample from 273 to 102 draw calls (raster/readback median 26.5 to 19.4 ms). All-type batching differed at 4–5 pixels by at most 1/255 and is not the default. This is not hardware performance evidence. A prior #20 SwiftShader run had an approximately 5.9-second composite stall that did not recur in later runs; its cause is unproven.

## Latest performance research and experiments

- External guidance consulted: MDN WebGL best practices (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices) recommends measuring DOM texture uploads, avoiding blocking GPU queries, and preallocating textures where appropriate; Chromium's canvas/WebGL discussion (https://issues.chromium.org/issues/41311312) explains why direct canvas upload may enable GPU copies and explicit readback can defeat those optimizations. These are hypotheses, not proof of the cause of our stalls.
- The C compositor uploads the union of current and previously marked influence bounds and clears stale data; it still samples the whole real world and all marked field texels. Its GPU output uses an influence-bounded scissor, while scalar fallback deliberately retains the full source window for displaced reads. Compared with the preserved immediately previous packed GPU path, 18 interleaved/moving/shrinking/empty stress cases and 42 transformed effect samples were byte-identical on SwiftShader; the previous older float-texture reference differed by 1/255 in one newly sampled #07 edge pixel, so it is not claimed bit-identical for all cases. Small fixed-frame paired medians improved by ~0.2–1.9ms in this software sample, not a device FPS claim.
- A natural #20 High sample after both changes reached 6.8s source time with median frame interval 60.2ms, p95 140.3ms and max 3.29s. Another intrusive profiling run measured an 8.97s first GPU-constructor stall; instrumented numbers are not directly comparable to baseline. Deferred WebGL work and software context creation remain unresolved, so these samples do not establish the cause or readiness.
- #19 sprite-only batches matched unbatched raster output for 294 alternating source ticks on SwiftShader. High stays unbatched; Balance and Performance now select the sampled sprite optimization, with a restore override. A natural-time Balance sample still had median 86.1ms, p95 117.6ms and max 4.3s, and it cannot establish a hardware benefit or source-faithful D19 A.
