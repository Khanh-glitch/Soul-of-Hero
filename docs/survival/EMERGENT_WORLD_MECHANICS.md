## Chains and player options

### 1. Tool → burning source → risk, material and routes

A real torch swing against a reachable live tree can ignite it in dry weather and consumes one torch durability on the committed ignition; the existing fire visuals and damage apply. A burning tree displays a reachable **E: douse** prompt. One actual charge from an owned carried flask/canteen is debited exactly once, subject to distance and clear collision segment; a failed, duplicate, empty-flask or blocked attempt cannot consume water. Water otherwise used to drink or garden is a real opportunity cost. Alternatively cut it before it spreads, leave/avoid it, or let rain/storm extinguish it. A tree fully cut while burning now cancels its fire; partially harvested wood stays depleted and remaining charred stock is capped by what actually remained. A completed burn still turns a living wood source into the existing physical charcoal source, and may leave burnt ground. It does not grant anything remotely.

Fire no longer spreads on a frame accumulator only while online. Each new burning source schedules one spread at 3 seconds in the shared world clock, with stable per-cell selection (55%, tunable), four cardinal directions and distances one or two tiles. Two-tile spread requires real grass/meadow/forest in between and does not cross a cleared source, burnt ground, water, snow, road, stone floor, blocking structure, wall or roof on that intervening cell. Empty vegetation may be scorched but does not itself propagate. At most two generations from the originating fire may schedule spread; further ignitions can burn but cannot schedule another wave. Source stock, collision and selected surface matter, not zone identity or a fabricated wind/climate map. Rain/storm wins if simultaneous with spread or burnout. Separate player-caused ignitions each have their own bound. The radius bound applies to this fire chain, not a claim that all possible world damage is globally bounded.

### 2. Fire → scarred soil → production choice

Fire reaching an empty flammable cell can scorch the **existing** garden's ground. A plot on `T.BURNT` runs at 55% of its otherwise computed environmental rate; its already planted crop and water are not duplicated or reset. The same environment function is used by manual growth and Tender maturity, so an offline event boundary changes the rate **from that instant**, not retrospectively. The garden panel and Tender status name scorched soil and show the resulting rate. On an owned plot within reach, the player may consume **2 fiber + 1 Bitterleaf** to restore the modified terrain to its original underlying cell; crop progress, source depletion and charred tree state remain unchanged. Or accept slower growth, cultivate elsewhere, use the output for non-farming purposes, or ignore farming entirely. Restoring an unmodified/non-scorched plot, temporary terrain or inaccessible plot fails without debit. No item or building is supplied for free.

## Timeline, save and caveats

Spread, ground scarring, burnout, weather slots and Tender maturity/dryness use the same event-split world clock both online and during offline settlement. Watering/restoration and cutting are normal player transactions at their committed instant. Existing saved `fire` values without new spread metadata complete/extinguish normally but do **not** get retroactive spread invented; new fires save additive `fireSpreadAt`/`fireDepth` state and validation. Prior source layout/UID/custody rules are unchanged. Reverting new saves to an old build is unsupported.

This is deliberately **not** a complete wildfire simulator: there is no wind vector, dynamic moisture diffusion, building combustion, general vegetation regrowth or offline per-frame actor damage/creature combat replay. Snow and stone break the particular propagation path; rain is the existing global weather, not an authored regional climate. Flame art is small at 640×360; the prompt supplies explicit identification, and garden controls still scroll. Soil compost cost and 55% factor are provisional, not real ecology. Simulation checks do not establish fun or long-run balance.
