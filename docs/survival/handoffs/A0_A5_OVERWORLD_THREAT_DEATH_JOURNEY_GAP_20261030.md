## Current addendum — first-hit score and bounded experimental mitigation

CT's later 2026-10-30 authority expressly permits one isolated A0-developed A5-workstream Skeleton candidate; the historical request below predates that decision. The candidate is **not mounted in production** and this addendum is not acceptance.

Source inspection found the earlier “zero-score enemy” wording was incorrect: `score_reward = 0` suppresses the Skeleton's kill bonus only. `game/characters/knight/knight.gd` adds damage to `GameSession.score` on 11 weapon/ability paths, and `game/heroes/ultimate/wizard_summoned_enemy.gd` awards score on its confirmed hits; even a nonlethal, leashed-and-rearmed enemy could otherwise yield repeat score. The previous real-death tests did not attack the enemy and cannot prove anti-farming. Score is displayed/published and included in a dungeon completion result; in the inspected `AppFlow.complete_active_run`, campaign rewards are requested by level/run ID, not by that score. This source observation does not prove the absence of every indirect XP/reward path. Dungeon `game/main.gd` grants XP through its separate tracked-enemy death handler; the test-composed overworld candidate is not connected to that handler, but an actual kill/reload XP and inventory check remains required.

Code `e256a718` attempts an owner-contained mitigation without changing shared Hero, Gold/VFX, or dungeon AI: the first actual health reduction synchronously commits one fixed `SaveService.transact_once` receipt, after which this candidate never rearms, including after a successful reload. Incoming-only encounters may still rearm after the existing cooldown. One normal encounter can still award the Hero's ordinary damage score, and the first hit consumes the encounter even if the player retreats; neither effect is product-approved. On a failed receipt write the live instance is removed and that session cannot rearm, but a later reload after a failed write could permit another score-bearing hit. Thus failure/restart is **not proven fail-closed** and no anti-farming acceptance or production mounting is warranted. Site and pre-hit cooldown are also not durable. Do not call this zero score or a complete persistence design.

## Why the missing journey cannot be honestly filled with the currently mounted world

- `game/overworld/chapter1_overworld.gd` builds the authored overworld, player, landmarks, camps, discoveries, survival resources/structures, death bags, UI and economy session. The `_spawn_player` comment explicitly characterizes the region as a travel space rather than a combat arena. In the inspected production overworld files there is no mounted enemy-spawn path.
- The existing world-data hazard rectangles are passed into `OverworldState.tick`; `_drain` only consumes provisions. `_apply_fatigue` says an empty pack slows movement and nothing else about the run is taken away; these hazards do not by themselves establish damage or lethal threat.
- Existing `game/characters/enemies/skeleton_enemy.gd` does implement chase and real damage, but an enemy class is not an authored overworld encounter: its existence does not authorize a spawn point, balance, navigation or lifecycle in this region. `game/world/hazards/spikes.gd` can damage the player, but explicitly defaults to owner `dungeon`, tags itself `dungeon_hazards`, and uses dungeon spike art. Neither is evidence of a mounted overworld danger or permission to insert one. `MULTI_AGENT_EXECUTION.md` assigns encounters/hazards/AI to A5, with A1 anchors and A2 collision dependencies; CT must decide the present integration authority rather than A0 silently spawning a dungeon actor.
- The production overworld death-bag path responds to the actual overworld Hero's global death event, and its respawn/ownership transaction already exists. The production dungeon uses its own scene, Hero and checkpoint; silently treating a dungeon death as an overworld bag would violate that isolation.
- This is source inspection and scoped technical evidence, not a formal proof that no possible third-party or future code can ever damage the Hero. In particular, no actual threat → overworld death → body bag → respawn → recovery/reload ordinary-input run has been demonstrated.
