# Veyra overworld — compiled from the gold map + the Drive tilesets, walk-testable

This folder holds **the overworld as a playable artefact**: one 8000 × 5600 pixel map
compiled from the gold map of branch `map/veyra-gold-asset-canon-20261102`, plus a
browser walk test that drives the real D051 avatar over it with real collision.

Nothing in `assets/` is hand-placed. Everything is produced by two scripts and can be
regenerated byte-for-byte (fixed seed, no randomness outside the seed).

```
python3 tools/world/materials.py        # verify the 16 px ground library (~1 s)
python3 tools/world/veyra_compile.py    # compile the world PNG + collision (~6 s)
```

## Run the walk test

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8008 --bind 0.0.0.0 --directory world   # repo root
# open http://<host>:8008/  (redirects to the walk test)
```

WASD / arrows walk, **Shift** runs, **M** toggles the minimap, **1–4** jump to the four
authored spawns. The viewport is a 640 × 360 integer-zoom window chasing the avatar.

Headless verification of the same file (no browser needed):

```bash
node tools/world/walktest.mjs http://127.0.0.1:8008/web/
```

## Scale — derived from the map and the assets, not from the clock

The gold raster is 250 × 175 cells at 8 world units per cell. The kits in the Drive
folder are 16 px tiles. The smallest feature the gold map commits to is a 2.5–4 unit
river — half a gold cell — so **one gold cell = 2 tiles**:

| quantity | value |
| --- | --- |
| tiles | 500 × 350 |
| pixels | 8000 × 5600 |
| pixels per gold unit | 4 |
| D051 body | ~22 px tall |
| avatar ÷ world width | 1 / 364 |

That ratio is the *Last Island Unknown 15 days* relationship the brief asked for: an
authored ~8000 px world against a character a bit over 20 px tall. The 170.7 s crossing
arithmetic from the old A1 world was deliberately **not** used as an input — the size
comes from the gold geometry and the tile grid.

## Why each material is where it is

`tools/world/materials.py` scans the source sheets for 16 × 16 windows that are 100 %
opaque (so they seam without patching alpha) **and** pass a colour-class test. A family
that cannot produce a tile raises `SystemExit` rather than falling back to something
invented — that is why the library is small and boring, and why it can be trusted.

| family | tiles | source | acceptance test | why here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `grass` | 8 | roads kit `Ground_grass.png` | green > 55 %, lum 70–150 | lowland plains, lake and river banks |
| `grass_dark` | 2 | same | lum 95–116 only | forest floor and thin-soil hillsides — the darker cut reads as canopy shade |
| `road` | 8 | roads kit `Road1_grass.png` | grey/cool cobble, lum 120–145 | **why cobbles and not dirt**: every settlement on the gold map sits on a road, so the road is paved where people have been and compacted earth everywhere else |
| `soil` | 6 | fields `FieldsTile_02/05/11` | warm, no vegetation, high texture | cultivated ground inside farm / mill / grove yards only |
| `sand` | 1 (flat) | island `Tileset/Tiled.png` | median of the sheet's own sand pixels | beaches **only along the sea**; rivers and lakes keep grass banks |
| `stone` | 6 | island `Tileset/Tiled.png` | cool, mid-tone | high ground above the 75th elevation percentile |
| `blight` | 6 | cursed land `Ground.png` | warm-red, no green | zones whose gold soil says `xỉ` / `độc` / `tro` |
| `water` | 4 | island sheet, sampled flattish depths | blue > red + 45 | sea, lakes, rivers |

**Relief comes from the gold raster.** Elevation is read straight out of
`terrain.elev` and split at the 45th / 75th / 92nd *land* percentiles into
`hill` (thin soil, grass-dark, scattered rocks) → `high rock` (barren stone, small
rock-fan trees) → `summit` (stone, no canopy, mineral rocks). That is why the
mountains sit where they sit and why the tree line stops at a consistent height —
it is the gold map's own relief, not a design guess.

**Props are y-sorted with a 1-tile foot collider**, anchored so the sprite's base sits
on its tile. Families: `tree_meadow / tree_forest / tree_rock / tree_dead`,
`bush, rock, rock_big, ruin, house, stall, crate, crop`. Which family grows in which
zone is the grammar table below — one line per gold zone type.

| gold zone (soil / tag) | ground | trees | decal density | prop density |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `đất rừng` / `trong rừng` | grass_dark | tree_forest | .16 | .055 |
| `nền đá` / `đá` | stone | tree_rock | .05 | .020 |
| `đất mùn` + hill (45–75 pct) | grass_dark | tree_rock | .09 | .018 |
| summit (> 92 pct) | stone | none | .10 | .010 |
| `ven biển` + `cát` | sand (sea band only) | none | .06 | .010 |
| `cát cứng` | sand | tree_dead | .05 | .012 |
| `xỉ` / `độc` / `tro` | blight | tree_dead | .07 | .022 |
| `bờ hồ` | grass | tree_meadow | .14 | .030 |
| `ven sông` | grass | tree_meadow | .12 | .028 |
| everything else | grass | tree_meadow | .10 | .020 |

A **POI yard is a plaza**: a rectangular pad of stone or field soil, structures ringed at
0.8 × the yard radius, and *no* scatter of any kind inside — so every settlement has a
walkable centre instead of being buried by its own trees. 80 of the 81 gold POIs now
have a walkable centre tile.

**River crossings are fords, not bridges.** The gold map declares zero bridges and the
bridges kit in the Drive folder is a module atlas that would need an autotile pass; a
road that meets water therefore crosses only where the wet run is ≤ 36 tiles, and that
crossing is drawn as shallow water you can wade and marked walkable. Longer road runs
over open water are sea routes and stay impassable.

## What was verified, and what was not

Verified in this sandbox (`node tools/world/walktest.mjs`, all checks passing):

- the page loads `world.png`, `world_meta.json` and the D051 walk sheet with no network;
- all four authored spawns resolve onto walkable ground;
- 360 simulated seconds of held-direction movement **never** enters a solid tile;
- collision, facing, run, minimap toggle, HUD and spawn cycling all behave;
- physics sanity: walk 96 px/s, run ×1.9.

Map facts from the compiled collision grid:

- 31.9 % of the canvas is walkable; the sea is everything else;
- the mainland is one component of 48 152 tiles (9 components in total: mainland,
  four large islands, four rocks);
- POI centres walkable **80/81**; POIs reachable **on foot from a spawn 58/81**.
  The 23 that are not are harbours, piers, lighthouses and island sites
  (`port, pier, warehouse, quarry, mine, gate, grove, ruins, elder_site, …`) — they are
  reached by boat in the gold design, and no sea route is in scope for a walk test.

**Not verified, and not claimable from here:** native Godot 4.7 rendering, native input
and collision, and any "this looks right" judgement on your behalf. Every image in
`preview/` is a Pillow composite labelled as a preview; `world/preview/spawns_2x2.png`
is the same 640 × 360 framing the browser shows, four spawns side by side.

## Known gaps

1. **Stone edge tiles.** The `stone` family still contains a few island-sheet tiles that
   read as cliff/wall edges (the pale crenellated strips at 1:1). They are used as flat
   ground in high-rock zones.
2. **No bridge sprites.** Fords cover short crossings; a real bridge pass needs the
   bridges kit's module atlas and an autotile table (16 px, `668008-bridges`).
3. **Water is flat.** The island sheet has no flat 16 × 16 water window, so sea colour is
   two depths sampled from the sheet itself plus a shallow shelf near land. Shore foam
   and animated water are still missing.
4. **Yards are rectangles.** Corners are visible at map zoom; an ellipse or a mask
   erode would soften them.
5. **Road junctions.** Paved roads meet at plain right angles with no junction modules
   from the roads kit.

## Layout

```
world/assets/     world.png (3.7 MB), world_meta.json (collision RLE + POIs + zones),
                  minimap.png, hero_walk.png, hero_idle.png (D051, 64 px cells)
world/web/        index.html + game.js — the walk test
world/preview/    overview.png (1:5), spawns_2x2.png (1:1), one file per spawn
tools/world/      materials.py, veyra_compile.py, walktest.mjs, data/, out/
```

`world_meta.json` schema `soh.veyra.world.v1`: `scale` (see table above), `spawns`,
`pois` (with `cell` in **tiles**), `resources`, `zones` (ring + grammar + gold soil /
tags), `lands`, `collision.encoding = "rle-[start,len,value]-per-row"` over 350 rows of
500 tiles.
